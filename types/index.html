types of meditation

here are some meditations that you can incorporate into your everyday life.

box breathing

box breathing has four equal sides, just like a square. each side is one part of your breath, and each part lasts 4 seconds.

1. Get settled: Sit up comfortably with your feet on the floor. Let your shoulders relax.

2. Breathe in for 4: Slowly breathe in through your nose while counting 1… 2… 3… 4.

3. Hold for 4: Gently hold your breath for 4 counts. No squeezing, just pause.

4. Breathe out for 4: Slowly let the air out through your mouth for 4 counts.

5. Hold for 4: Pause again for 4 counts before your next breath in.

6. Repeat: That's one box! Try 4 rounds, tracing the square in your mind.

body scanning

a body scan is like saying hi to every part of yourself. just notice.

1. Get comfy: Lie down or lean back in a chair. Close your eyes or let your gaze go soft.

2. Start at your toes: Notice your feet. Are they warm, cold, tingly, or tired?

3. Drift upward: Slowly move your attention up through your legs, hips, and belly, spending a few breaths on each part.

4. Chest, shoulders, arms: Notice where you're holding tension. Shoulders love to creep up toward your ears. Let them drop.

5. Face and head: Relax your jaw, your forehead, the space between your eyebrows.

take one big breath and notice your whole body at once. how do you feel now?

body movement

not every meditation means sitting still. sometimes you need to move to feel calm.

1. Shake it out: Stand up and shake your hands, arms, and legs for about 20 seconds. Be silly with it.

2. Roll your shoulders and neck: Slowly roll your shoulders back a few times, then gently tilt your head side to side.

3. Reach and stretch: Reach your arms up toward the ceiling, then lean gently to each side.

4. Sway: Let your body sway slowly, like a plant in the breeze, while you breathe.

5. Stand still: Stop and notice. Your heartbeat, your breath, how your body feels after moving.

move gently and skip anything that doesn't feel good. your body, your rules.

gratitude

gratitude meditation is about noticing the good stuff, even on hard days.

1. Settle in: Sit comfortably and take three slow breaths.

2. Think of a person: Picture someone you're thankful for. What do you appreciate about them?

3. Think of something small: A good snack, a song you love, sunlight through the window. Tiny things count.

4. Think of yourself: What's one thing you're proud of, or one way you took care of yourself today?

5. Feel it: Notice how your body feels when you think about these things. Warm? Lighter? Just notice.

want to make it a habit? try writing down three good things before bed.
